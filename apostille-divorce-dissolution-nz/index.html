What are New Zealand divorce papers called?

The legal term in New Zealand is dissolution of a marriage or civil union, and the Family Court ends it with a Dissolution Order. Overseas offices may ask for a divorce certificate, divorce decree or final divorce papers. They don't always mean the same document, and some will accept a different document showing the marriage has ended. We confirm what yours will accept before anything is lodged.

Will my copy be accepted?

Often not. Only certain forms of divorce papers can be apostilled, and a printout of an emailed order, a photocopy or an older document that doesn't show the divorce are all refused, usually weeks later. Send us what you have and we'll tell you straight away whether it will work, and what to do if it won't.

Frequently asked questions

Does New Zealand issue a divorce certificate?

No. The Family Court issues a Dissolution Order. That is usually what overseas offices mean by a divorce certificate or decree, and we confirm what yours will accept.

Can NZ divorce papers be apostilled?

Yes, but only in certain forms. We check your copy before anything is lodged and tell you what to do if it will not be accepted.

Can I apostille a printout of my emailed Dissolution Order?

No. A printout of an emailed or downloaded document is refused. Send us what you have and we will tell you what is needed instead.
