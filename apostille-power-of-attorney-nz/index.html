How to Apostille a New Zealand Power of Attorney

Using a New Zealand power of attorney overseas? It may need notarisation first, followed by an apostille or authentication depending on the destination country.

Quick answer: If a New Zealand power of attorney is being used overseas and the receiving organisation requires an apostille, the current Department of Internal Affairs Authentication Unit requirements state that the power of attorney must first be notarised by a New Zealand Notary Public. After notarisation, it can proceed through the appropriate apostille or authentication pathway for the destination country.

Important: NZ Document Assist does not draft powers of attorney or provide legal advice. We provide administrative document assistance and can coordinate the notary, apostille, authentication, translation and document-return steps after you have the appropriate document.

Does a New Zealand power of attorney need notarisation before apostille?

Yes, for the Department of Internal Affairs apostille/authentication process. The Authentication Unit's current document requirements specifically list a power of attorney among documents that must be notarised by a New Zealand Notary Public.

This is important because ordinary certification is not enough for this particular DIA requirement.

Can a Justice of the Peace certify it instead?

Not for a power of attorney that is being prepared for the DIA apostille or authentication process. Current New Zealand Government guidance says that where a document needs notarisation for this process, you must use a New Zealand Notary Public. A Justice of the Peace or ordinary solicitor certification will not satisfy that requirement.

See our JP vs Notary Public NZ guide for a clearer explanation of the difference.

What does the Notary Public need to do?

For documents being notarised for apostille or authentication, current DIA guidance says the notarial act must describe what the notary has done, such as witnessing a signature or certifying a true copy. It must also include the notary's name and signature, seal or stamp, the date and place of signing, and make clear that all pages of the document have been seen.

When arranging the appointment, tell the notary that the document is being notarised for a New Zealand apostille or authentication.

How to apostille a New Zealand power of attorney

  1. Confirm the overseas requirements. Ask the receiving organisation exactly what form of power of attorney it needs and whether apostille or authentication is required.
  2. Have the document properly prepared. If legal drafting or advice is required, use an appropriately qualified lawyer.
  3. Arrange NZ Notary Public notarisation. The power of attorney must meet DIA's notarisation requirements before it can proceed.
  4. Confirm the destination pathway. The country determines whether apostille, Category A authentication or Category B authentication applies.
  5. Submit the notarised document to DIA. Complete the current Authentication Unit application and include the required documents and payment.
  6. Arrange final delivery. Follow any special instructions from the overseas organisation, embassy or authority.

Apostille or authentication for a power of attorney?

The document itself does not determine this. The destination country does.

If the document is being used in a country on New Zealand's apostille list, the apostille pathway applies. Other countries may require Category A or Category B authentication. Category B can also involve an overseas embassy and has a more complex process.

See our Apostille vs Authentication NZ guide.

Why is the overseas organisation's wording so important?

Powers of attorney are often used for significant transactions such as property, banking, company matters or representation overseas. The receiving lawyer, bank, registry, government office or other authority may have very specific requirements about signatures, witnesses, wording, notarisation, translation, apostille format and document age.

An apostille confirms the authenticity of the relevant New Zealand signature, stamp or seal. It does not confirm that the underlying power of attorney is legally suitable for the transaction in the overseas country.

General power of attorney vs enduring power of attorney

Do not assume these are interchangeable. New Zealand law has specific requirements for enduring powers of attorney, including prescribed execution and witnessing requirements. If you are creating, changing or relying on an enduring power of attorney, legal advice may be required.

Our service begins with administrative coordination of an existing document; we do not advise you which type of power of attorney you should create.

Paper apostille or e-apostille?

New Zealand offers both paper and electronic apostilles. The receiving organisation should tell you which format it accepts.

For transactions involving original signed documents, overseas property or registries, it is especially important to check whether physical originals and a paper apostille are required before choosing an e-apostille.

See our Paper Apostille vs E-Apostille NZ guide.

Current government apostille fee and timeframe

The current New Zealand Government fee is NZ$37 for one apostille certificate, paper or electronic.

Standard apostille processing is currently within 15 working days. Eligible urgent apostille and e-apostille applications are processed within 5 working days for an additional government urgent-service fee of NZ$120.

The Notary Public's professional fee is separate, as are translation, courier and administrative-assistance costs where applicable.

What if the power of attorney needs translation?

Check the overseas organisation's translation requirements before arranging one. DIA can arrange translation as part of an application, or translation can be organised independently.

If you arrange your own translation for the DIA process, current government guidance says the translation must itself be notarised by a New Zealand Notary Public.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Getting the power of attorney certified by a JP instead of notarised by an NZ Notary Public for the DIA process.
  • Getting an apostille before confirming that the destination country actually uses apostilles.
  • Assuming the apostille proves the power of attorney is legally valid for the intended overseas transaction.
  • Signing the document before checking whether the overseas authority or notary has specific witnessing requirements.
  • Choosing an e-apostille when the overseas registry or lawyer requires physical originals.
  • Arranging translation without checking how the translation must be notarised or presented.

Frequently asked questions

Can a New Zealand power of attorney be apostilled?

Yes, if apostille is the correct pathway for the destination country and the document meets DIA requirements. Current Authentication Unit requirements state that a power of attorney must first be notarised by a New Zealand Notary Public.

Can a JP notarise my power of attorney for apostille?

No. Where DIA requires notarisation for apostille or authentication, current government guidance requires a New Zealand Notary Public rather than a Justice of the Peace or ordinary solicitor certification.

Does NZ Document Assist write powers of attorney?

No. We provide administrative document assistance and coordination only. We do not draft powers of attorney or provide legal advice.

Does an apostille make my power of attorney valid overseas?

An apostille verifies the relevant signature, stamp or seal. Whether the underlying document is legally suitable for the intended overseas transaction is a separate matter that should be confirmed with the receiving organisation or an appropriately qualified legal professional.

How much is the government apostille fee?

The current government apostille fee is NZ$37 for one certificate. Notary, translation, courier and assistance fees are separate where applicable.
