What Is an Apostille in New Zealand?

An apostille is an official certificate used to help verify the origin of certain New Zealand public documents when they are being presented in another country that recognises the Hague Apostille Convention.

In New Zealand, apostilles are issued through the Department of Internal Affairs Authentication Unit. NZ Document Assist does not issue apostilles. We provide independent administrative assistance and coordination to help make the process easier to manage.

Important: whether you need an apostille, authentication, notarisation or another step depends on your document and the country where it will be used.

Documents Commonly Used Overseas

People seek apostille assistance for many different types of New Zealand documents. The exact requirements depend on the receiving country and organisation.

  • New Zealand birth certificates
  • Marriage and civil union certificates
  • Death certificates
  • Academic and education documents
  • Police or background-related documents
  • Company and business documents
  • Powers of attorney and privately prepared documents
  • Certified copies and documents requiring notarisation
  • Documents required for overseas property, banking or estate matters

Not every document follows the same process. Some documents can be submitted directly, while others may first need certification, notarisation or another preliminary step.

How Long Does an Apostille Take in New Zealand?

The New Zealand Government currently advises allowing up to 15 working days for standard apostille processing.

An eligible urgent service may be available, with government processing within approximately 5 working days. The Government currently charges an additional NZ$120 urgent-service fee where the urgent option applies.

Government processing times are outside our control and can change. Our priority service relates to our own administrative handling and does not guarantee faster processing by government agencies, notaries, translators, embassies or courier providers.

Apostille or Authentication — Which Do You Need?

These terms are often confused, but they are not the same process. The correct route largely depends on the country where the New Zealand document will be used.

Apostille Authentication
Generally used for countries that recognise the Hague Apostille Convention. Often used where the destination country does not use the Hague Apostille process.
Usually involves an apostille certificate issued in New Zealand. May involve authentication followed by additional embassy, consular or foreign-government requirements.

If your document requires authentication rather than an apostille, see our New Zealand document authentication service.

What If My Document Needs a Notary?

Some documents must be notarised before they can proceed through the apostille or authentication process.

Where notarisation is required as part of the New Zealand apostille/authentication process, the Department of Internal Affairs specifies that the relevant notarisation must be performed by a New Zealand Notary Public.

NZ Document Assist is not a Notary Public, but we can help coordinate an appointment and document handling. See our notary coordination service.

Frequently Asked Questions About Apostilles in NZ

What is an apostille in New Zealand?

An apostille is an official certificate used to help verify the origin of certain New Zealand public documents for use in countries that recognise the Hague Apostille Convention.

Who issues apostilles in New Zealand?

Apostilles are issued through the New Zealand Department of Internal Affairs Authentication Unit. NZ Document Assist provides independent administrative assistance and does not issue apostilles.

How much is an apostille in New Zealand?

The Government currently charges NZ$37 for one apostille certificate. Our separate apostille coordination fee starts from NZ$89. Other costs may apply depending on your document and requirements.

How long does an apostille take?

The New Zealand Government currently advises allowing up to 15 working days for standard processing. Eligible urgent applications may be processed within approximately 5 working days for an additional government fee.

Can I get an apostille urgently?

An urgent Government service is available for eligible applications. The current additional Government urgent fee is NZ$120. Eligibility and government processing times are controlled by the relevant authority rather than NZ Document Assist.

Do I need an apostille or authentication?

It depends on the destination country and the document involved. Countries using the Hague Apostille Convention generally accept apostilles, while other countries may require authentication and additional steps.

Can NZ Document Assist give me legal or immigration advice?

No. We provide administrative document assistance and coordination only. We are not a law firm, Notary Public, government agency or licensed immigration adviser.
