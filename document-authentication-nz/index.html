What is Document Authentication?

When your destination country is outside the Hague Apostille Convention, standard apostilles do not apply. Instead, papers require multi-step document authentication or legalisation via the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) Authentication Unit to verify official signatures and seals.

Apostille vs. Authentication Overview

  • Apostille Pathway: Used for Hague Convention members (e.g., Australia, USA, UK) processed through the DIA.
  • Authentication Pathway: Required for non-member countries (e.g., China, UAE) and may involve the DIA, MFAT, and foreign embassies.

Category A vs. Category B Pathways

Category A

Involves the DIA Authentication Unit followed by MFAT verification, with standard processing taking up to 15 working days (or 5 working days for urgent requests). Common destinations include the UAE and Vietnam.

Category B

Requires DIA, MFAT, and physical embassy processing (such as for Iraq or Libya). Embassy rules vary widely with no fixed government timeframes.

Documents Processed & Key Traps Avoided

We handle standard records like birth certificates, educational credentials, and corporate assets while avoiding critical pitfalls:

  1. The JP Trap: Government authorities reject ordinary Justice of the Peace stamps for authentication; a New Zealand Notary Public is required.
  2. The Copy and Translation Traps: Overseas agencies often demand strict physical originals or specifically notarised translations.
