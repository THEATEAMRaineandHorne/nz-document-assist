What Is Document Authentication in New Zealand?

Document authentication is used when a New Zealand document needs to be formally verified for use in certain overseas countries.

It verifies the signature, stamp or seal on the document. The process is sometimes also referred to as legalisation.

In New Zealand, authentication is handled through the Department of Internal Affairs Authentication Unit. Some authentication cases also involve verification by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade (MFAT), and Category B cases can involve an overseas embassy.

Important:

Whether you need an apostille or authentication depends primarily on the country where your documents will be used. If you are not sure which route applies, tell us the destination and we can help coordinate the practical next steps.

Apostille or Authentication — Which One Do I Need?

These terms are often confused, but the processes are different.

Apostille Authentication
Generally used for countries participating in the Hague Apostille Convention process. Used for countries that require authentication or legalisation instead.
Does not normally require MFAT verification. Category A involves MFAT verification and Category B can involve additional embassy processing.
Available as paper or electronic apostille. Authentication is generally handled as a physical document process.

If your destination uses the apostille route, see our Apostille NZ service .

Category A vs Category B Authentication

New Zealand authentication cases are divided into Category A and Category B depending on the country where the documents will be used.

Category A Authentication

The documents are first authenticated through the Department of Internal Affairs Authentication Unit and then sent to MFAT for verification.

After verification, the authenticated documents are returned using the delivery details provided with the application.

Category B Authentication

Category B is more involved. The documents can require DIA and MFAT processing plus authentication or legalisation through an overseas embassy.

Embassy requirements can vary, including payment arrangements, forms and return-delivery instructions.

Some organisations in Category B countries may accept Category A authentication instead. If you have been given specific instructions by the receiving organisation, send them to us so we can take them into account before coordinating the process.

Which Countries Use Category A or Category B Authentication?

The authentication category depends on the destination country and can change over time.

For example, current Category A destinations include countries such as Algeria, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Egypt, Ghana, Iran, Jordan, Kenya, Kuwait, Lebanon, Malaysia, Nepal, Nigeria and Papua New Guinea.

Category B destinations currently include countries such as Afghanistan, Benin, Cameroon, Ethiopia, Haiti, Iraq, Jamaica, Libya and North Korea.

Rather than trying to determine the category yourself, simply tell us the country where the documents will be used and we can help identify the practical authentication route.

What Documents May Need Authentication?

Authentication may be required for many different New Zealand documents, depending on the destination and what the overseas organisation requests.

  • birth, marriage and death certificates
  • education and qualification documents
  • criminal record documents
  • company and corporate documents
  • powers of attorney
  • estate and inheritance documents
  • property-related documents
  • privately prepared documents
  • documents used for overseas employment or registration
  • other documents requested by foreign authorities

Does My Document Need to Be an Original?

It depends on the document.

Some authentication applications require originals, while other documents may be accepted as properly notarised copies or in another permitted format.

The overseas organisation may also impose its own requirements. It may, for example, require a recently issued original even where another format might otherwise be accepted.

Unsure what to send?

Contact us before sending valuable originals or arranging certification. We can help coordinate the document requirements first.

What If My Document Needs to Be Notarised?

If notarisation is required for the New Zealand authentication process, the document must be notarised by a New Zealand Notary Public.

A Justice of the Peace or ordinary solicitor certification is not a substitute when this specific notarisation requirement applies.

NZ Document Assist is not a Notary Public, but we can help coordinate the notarial stage as part of the wider authentication process.

See our Notary Coordination service .

What If My Documents Need Translation?

Translation requirements depend on the receiving country and organisation.

If an independently arranged translation is being used as part of the authentication process, additional notarisation requirements can apply.

It is therefore better to establish the translation requirements before arranging the work.

We can help coordinate this through our Translation Coordination service .

How Long Does Document Authentication Take?

Category A

Standard Category A authentication processing is currently within 15 working days once a complete eligible application has been received.

Eligible urgent Category A applications can currently be processed within 5 working days.

An additional NZ$120 urgent-processing fee applies.

Category B

Category B cases can take longer because documents may need to move through DIA, MFAT and an overseas embassy.

There is currently no fixed timeframe for the MFAT and embassy stages of Category B authentication.

Working to a deadline?

Contact us early. Category B cases in particular can involve several organisations, embassy requirements and courier stages.

Can Authentication Be Done Urgently?

Eligible Category A authentication applications can use the current urgent service.

To qualify, the application must be complete and correct, contain all required documents and must not require translation.

Category B authentication does not have the same 5-working-day urgent processing arrangement because of the additional MFAT and embassy stages.

Category B Authentication — Why Is It More Complicated?

Category B cases often involve more than one organisation.

Depending on the destination, the embassy may have its own requirements about:

  • which documents it needs
  • additional forms or letters
  • fees and payment method
  • courier or return-envelope arrangements
  • how the documents must be presented

This is where coordination can be particularly useful, because the authentication is only one part of the complete document journey.

Common Problems We Help Clients Avoid

  • Applying for an apostille when the destination actually requires authentication.
  • Assuming Category A and Category B follow the same process.
  • Sending a copy when an original is required.
  • Having a document certified by a JP when a Notary Public is required.
  • Arranging a translation before understanding how it must be prepared.
  • Missing an embassy payment or document-return requirement.
  • Assuming Category B has a predictable completion date.
  • Not allowing enough time for MFAT, embassy and international courier stages.

Document Authentication NZ — Common Questions

What is document authentication in New Zealand?

It is a formal process that verifies the signature, stamp or seal on certain New Zealand documents for overseas use. Authentication is sometimes also called legalisation.

What is Category A authentication?

Category A documents are authenticated through the Department of Internal Affairs Authentication Unit and then sent to MFAT for verification.

What is Category B authentication?

Category B authentication is a more involved process that can require DIA and MFAT processing plus authentication or legalisation through an overseas embassy.

How long does Category A authentication take?

Standard Category A processing is currently within 15 working days once a complete eligible application is received. Eligible urgent Category A applications can currently be processed within 5 working days.

How long does Category B authentication take?

There is no fixed timeframe for the MFAT and embassy stages of a Category B case. The overall time depends on the organisations involved and the destination country's requirements.

Can I use a Justice of the Peace?

Not where the document specifically needs to be notarised for the authentication process. In that situation, a New Zealand Notary Public is required.

Do I need to contact an embassy for Category B authentication?

Category B can involve an overseas embassy, which may have its own requirements for documents, payment and return delivery. We can help coordinate the practical steps around those requirements.

Can I arrange authentication while living overseas?

In many cases, yes. Depending on the documents and country, the process can often be coordinated using email, post and international courier services.

What if I am not sure whether I need an apostille or authentication?

Tell us the document type, destination country and any instructions you have received. We can help identify the practical administrative route that needs to be checked.
