How we help

In New Zealand, copies can be certified by a Justice of the Peace, a lawyer, a court Registrar or a Notary Public, and the organisation receiving the copy decides which it accepts. Documents going overseas are where it gets tricky: many need a Notary Public before they can be apostilled, and a JP or ordinary lawyer's certification is refused. Certified copy, notarised copy, apostille: what's the difference?

TermWhat it is
Certified copyA copy someone authorised has checked against the original and endorsed as true
Notarised copyA formal act by a Notary Public, with their seal, recognised overseas
ApostilleA government certificate that verifies the seal or signature, so the document is accepted in Hague Convention countries
AuthenticationThe equivalent for countries outside the Hague Convention, a longer process

Common mistakes

  • Getting a copy certified before checking who the receiving office accepts
  • Assuming a JP copy will work overseas
  • Using an ordinary lawyer when the instructions say "notarised"
  • Paying to certify a document that should have gone as the original
  • Certifying only part of a document that needed every page done

Frequently asked questions

Who can certify documents in NZ?

A Justice of the Peace, a lawyer, a court Registrar or Deputy Registrar, or a Notary Public. The organisation receiving the copy decides which it accepts.

Is it free to get documents certified by a JP?

Yes. JPs certify copies free of charge, and for many uses within New Zealand that is all you need.

Can a JP certify documents for overseas use?

Often not. Many documents going overseas must be notarised by a Notary Public before they can be apostilled, depending on the document and the country. We check yours and book the notary if one is needed.

How much does a Notary Public charge in NZ?

Notaries set their own fees. When we arrange the notary for you, it is included in one all-in quote with the apostille, before any work starts.

Does a birth certificate need certifying before an apostille?

Not usually. What a document needs, whether the original, a new certificate or a certified copy, depends on the document and the country. Tell us both and we will tell you exactly what applies.
