Who Can Certify Documents in New Zealand?

JP, lawyer, court registrar or Notary Public: who can certify a copy, when a JP is enough, and when a document going overseas needs a notary instead.

Quick answer

In New Zealand, copies are usually certified by a Justice of the Peace (free), a lawyer, a court Registrar or Deputy Registrar or a Notary Public. The organisation receiving the copy decides which of them it accepts. For a document going overseas with an apostille, DIA requires a NZ Notary Public wherever notarisation is needed and refuses JP or solicitor certification. A notary typically charges NZ$100–175.

The Ministry of Justice lists JPs, Solicitors of the High Court, Notaries Public and Deputy Registrars as the people who commonly witness or certify documents for the courts. Other organisations publish their own lists, and some exclude certifiers others accept. The University of Auckland, for example, does not accept police officers for admissions documents.

What is a certified copy?

A copy the certifier has compared with the original and endorsed as a true copy, usually with wording such as “Certified true copy of the original sighted by me”, their name, signature, date and designation. If the organisation gives you its own wording, use that. Certification does not make the copy a new original.

When is a JP not enough?

When the document is going overseas and DIA requires it to be notarised before it can be apostilled or authenticated. DIA's rule is explicit: use a NZ Notary Public, not a JP or ordinary solicitor, or the document will not be apostilled. This applies to powers of attorney, school documents, wills, deeds, employment and medical records, private documents and translations you arrange yourself.

It does not apply to BDM certificates, criminal records or NZQA records. These go to DIA as originals and need no certification at all. See NZ Apostille Requirements.

Does every page need certifying?

Often yes. Many organisations want each page endorsed. For a notarial act used with an apostille, the notary must make clear they have seen every page of the document.

Common mistakes

  • Certifying a copy before checking who the receiving organisation accepts
  • Assuming a JP copy will work overseas
  • Using an ordinary lawyer when the instructions say “notarised”
  • Paying a notary to certify a BDM certificate that DIA wants as the original
  • Certifying only the first page when every page is required

Frequently asked questions

Who can certify documents in NZ?

A Justice of the Peace, a lawyer, a court Registrar or Deputy Registrar, or a Notary Public. The organisation receiving the copy decides which it accepts.

Is it free to get documents certified by a JP?

Yes. JPs certify copies and witness documents free of charge.

Can a JP certify documents for overseas use?

Sometimes, if the overseas organisation accepts it. But where DIA requires notarisation before an apostille or authentication, a JP certification is refused and you need a NZ Notary Public.

Can a police officer certify documents in NZ?

Some organisations accept it and some do not. The University of Auckland, for example, does not accept police officers for admissions documents. Check the organisation's own list.

How much does a Notary Public charge in NZ?

Notaries set their own fees, typically NZ$100 to NZ$175 for a straightforward notarisation. We book the appointment for NZ$39.

Does a birth certificate need certifying before an apostille?

No. DIA apostilles the original BDM certificate directly. A certified or notarised copy is not needed and not accepted.
