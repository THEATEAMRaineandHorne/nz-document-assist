Apostille for NZ Degrees, Diplomas and Education Documents

Taking a New Zealand qualification overseas? The receiving university, employer, professional body may ask for an apostille or another form of record checks.

NEW ZEALAND OVERSEAS DOCUMENTS GUIDE

Quick answer: A New Zealand degree, diploma, transcript or other record may need an apostille for overseas use. But an apostille is not always required. First ask the overseas body exactly what it needs. It may want an apostille, a document sent directly by your provider, online verification, or another form of proof.

When might an record need an apostille?

You may need to prove a record is genuine for work, study or registration overseas. An apostille can confirm that a New Zealand signature, stamp or seal on the document is genuine.

But some universities, employers and professional bodies use their own checks. They may ask for an official transcript from your university or provider. They may also accept an online record or another format.

Important: Before you pay for an apostille, ask the overseas organisation what it accepts. An apostille does not replace its own academic checks.

Which New Zealand records may be involved?

The overseas body may ask for one or more of these documents:

  • degree or qualification certificate
  • diploma
  • academic transcript
  • New Zealand Record of Achievement
  • NCEA or University Entrance documents
  • Overseas Results Notice
  • a letter or statement from an provider
  • another official academic record

Schools, universities and other providers can issue official records. NZQA can also provide some official qualification documents.

Step 1: Ask what the overseas organisation requires

This is the most important step. Ask for the requirements in writing if possible.

Check which document is needed, what format is accepted, how it must be sent, and how the organisation wants its authenticity confirmed.

For example, it may ask for an apostille. Or it may require the document to come directly from the school or provider. For a wider overview, see Documents for Overseas Use.

Step 2: Check whether you need apostille or authentication

The right route depends mainly on the country. Some countries use apostilles. Others use authentication.

If you are unsure, see our Apostille vs Authentication NZ guide.

Step 3: Identify the exact record

“My degree” can mean different things. It may mean the degree certificate, an academic transcript, an NZQA record or another document from your provider.

Ask the overseas body which document it wants before you pay for any certification or apostille work.

Step 4: Check the DIA rules

The DIA unit can only process records that meet its rules. The right steps depend on the record and who issued it.

You may need an original or an accepted copy. You may also need notarisation or another check. Check NZ Apostille Requirements before you prepare the document.

Degrees, diplomas and transcripts do not all follow one rule. Different issuers and formats can need different steps.

Do records always need a Notary Public first?

No. Do not arrange notarisation until you know it is required.

If the DIA unit requires notarisation, a New Zealand Notary Public must do it. A Justice of the Peace or ordinary solicitor certification is not a substitute when a New Zealand Notary Public is specifically required.

See our JP vs Notary Public NZ guide and Notary Coordination service.

NZQA documents and provider-issued documents

NZQA can provide official qualification records for some New Zealand study. Your school, university or provider can also supply official transcripts and other records.

For overseas use, the overseas body may accept direct supply, online verification, notarisation, apostille or authentication. The correct method depends on its rules and the document involved.

An apostille is not the same as academic recognition

An apostille checks a signature, stamp or seal. It does not decide whether your qualification is equal to a qualification in another country.

The overseas university, employer, professional body or recognition authority makes that decision.

Paper apostille or e-apostille?

New Zealand offers paper apostilles and e-apostilles. Ask the overseas body which format it accepts.

If it needs physical documents or a sealed package, paper delivery may suit the process. If it accepts an electronic apostille, an e-apostille may be suitable. See our Paper Apostille vs E-Apostille NZ guide.

Current apostille fees and processing times

The current New Zealand Government fee is NZ$37 for one apostille certificate, whether paper or electronic.

Standard processing is currently within 15 working days. Some urgent applications can be done within 5 working days. The additional government urgent-service fee is NZ$120.

Urgent processing is only available when the application meets the current rules. For example, the application must be complete and correct. Some applications that involve translation may not qualify.

What if the record needs translation?

If a translation is needed, first check the overseas organisation's rules. Also check the DIA rules before you arrange the translation.

For some translations prepared outside the government process, New Zealand Notary Public notarisation may be required. A translation can change the steps and timing.

See our Translation Coordination service.

Common mistakes with records

  • Apostilling a degree certificate when the organisation actually wants the transcript.
  • Paying for notarisation before checking whether it is needed.
  • Assuming an apostille proves academic recognition or equivalence.
  • Using a photocopy without checking whether that copy is acceptable.
  • Choosing an e-apostille when physical documents are required.
  • Failing to ask whether the university or provider must send the document directly.
  • Starting translation before checking the translation and notarisation rules.

A practical checklist before you start

  1. Identify the destination country.
  2. Ask the overseas body for its written requirements.
  3. Confirm exactly which record it wants.
  4. Ask whether the document must come directly from the institution.
  5. Check the DIA preparation rules for that document.
  6. Then arrange only the apostille, notary, translation and courier steps you actually need.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an apostille for my New Zealand degree?

Possibly, but not always. Ask the overseas organisation what it requires. It may want an apostille, direct verification from the school or provider, an official transcript, online verification or another process.

Should I apostille my degree or my transcript?

That depends on the overseas body. Ask it to name the exact document or documents it wants before you apply.

Does an apostille prove my NZ degree is recognised overseas?

No. An apostille confirms the relevant signature, stamp or seal. Recognition is a separate decision made overseas.

Can NZQA help with documents for using my qualification overseas?

Yes. NZQA provides official qualification documents and guidance for people using New Zealand qualifications overseas. Your school, university or provider may also need to provide official records.

How much is a New Zealand apostille?

The current government fee is NZ$37 for one apostille certificate. Other costs may apply for notary work, translation, courier services or admin help.

How long does an education-document apostille take?

Standard processing is currently within 15 working days. Eligible urgent applications are processed within 5 working days for an additional NZ$120 government urgent-service fee.
