Apostille for NZ Degrees, Diplomas and Education Documents

Taking a New Zealand qualification overseas? The receiving university, employer, professional body or authority may ask for an apostille or another form of document verification.

NEW ZEALAND OVERSEAS DOCUMENTS GUIDE

Quick answer: A New Zealand degree, diploma, transcript or other education document may need an apostille when it is being used overseas, but do not assume apostille is always the required route. NZQA advises people using NZ qualifications overseas to first check what the receiving organisation requires, including which documents it needs, the required format, and whether authenticity must be established by apostille, direct delivery from the issuing institution, online verification or another method.

When might an education document need an apostille?

You may be asked to authenticate education documents when applying for overseas employment, further study, professional registration or another official process. If the destination uses the apostille pathway, an apostille may be requested to confirm the relevant New Zealand signature, stamp or seal is genuine.

However, some universities, employers and professional bodies use their own verification systems. They may want an official transcript sent directly by your university or education provider, an online-verifiable document, or another specific format instead.

Important: Before paying for an apostille, ask the overseas organisation exactly what it accepts. An apostille is not a substitute for a receiving organisation's own academic-verification requirements.

Which New Zealand education documents may be involved?

Depending on the overseas application, you may be asked for one or more of the following:

  • degree or qualification certificate
  • diploma
  • academic transcript
  • New Zealand Record of Achievement
  • NCEA or University Entrance documentation
  • Overseas Results Notice
  • letter or statement from an education provider
  • other official academic records requested by the receiving organisation

NZQA notes that schools and tertiary providers can provide official transcripts and other documents, while some official qualification documents can be obtained through NZQA.

Step 1: Ask what the overseas organisation actually requires

This is the most important step. NZQA specifically recommends checking:

  • which documents are required
  • what format they must be supplied in
  • how the documents must arrive
  • what process must be followed for the documents to be recognised as authentic and original

For example, the organisation may require apostille certification, or it may instead require the document to be emailed or posted directly from the issuing authority. For the wider overseas-document process, see Documents for Overseas Use.

Step 2: Confirm whether the destination uses apostille or authentication

Whether a New Zealand document needs an apostille or authentication depends primarily on the country where it will be used. Apostille countries follow the apostille pathway; other destinations may require Category A or Category B authentication.

See our Apostille vs Authentication NZ guide before preparing documents if you are uncertain.

Step 3: Identify the exact education document

“My degree” can mean several different things: the physical degree certificate, an academic transcript, an NZQA record, or a provider-issued verification document. The receiving organisation may require one but not the others.

Getting this right at the beginning can avoid paying to apostille a document that the overseas organisation did not actually ask for.

Step 4: Check the Authentication Unit's document requirements

The Department of Internal Affairs Authentication Unit can only process documents that meet its requirements. Depending on the type and source of the education document, the appropriate preparation may involve an original document, an acceptable copy, notarisation, or another verification step. Review NZ Apostille Requirements before preparing the document.

We deliberately do not treat every degree, diploma and transcript as having the same preparation rule. Education documents come from different issuing organisations and can be supplied in different formats.

Do education documents always need a Notary Public first?

No. Do not automatically pay for notarisation before checking the document and receiving organisation's requirements.

If the Authentication Unit requires a document to be notarised for its process, the notarisation must be carried out by a New Zealand Notary Public. A Justice of the Peace or ordinary solicitor certification is not a substitute where the Authentication Unit specifically requires NZ Notary Public notarisation.

See our JP vs Notary Public NZ guide and Notary Coordination service if this step applies.

NZQA documents and provider-issued documents

NZQA can provide official qualification records for some New Zealand study. Education providers can also supply documents such as official transcripts, programme details and other records.

NZQA's current guidance also notes several ways education documents may be verified or authenticated for overseas use, including direct supply from NZQA, online verification, notarisation where appropriate, and apostille or authentication through the Department of Internal Affairs.

Apostille is not the same as academic recognition

This distinction matters. An apostille verifies the relevant signature, stamp or seal on the document. It does not decide whether an overseas university, employer or professional authority will recognise your qualification as equivalent to a qualification in its own country.

The receiving organisation or relevant overseas recognition authority makes that separate decision.

Paper apostille or e-apostille?

New Zealand offers both paper apostilles and e-apostilles. The correct choice depends on what the receiving organisation accepts.

If an overseas university requires physical documents or a sealed package, a paper/delivery pathway may be more appropriate. If it accepts electronically apostilled documents, an e-apostille may be suitable. See our Paper Apostille vs E-Apostille NZ guide.

Current apostille fees and processing times

The current New Zealand Government fee is NZ$37 for one apostille certificate, paper or electronic.

Current standard apostille processing is within 15 working days. Eligible urgent apostille and e-apostille applications are processed within 5 working days for an additional government urgent-service fee of NZ$120.

Urgent processing is only available when the application meets the current eligibility conditions, including being complete and correct and not requiring translation.

What if the education document needs translation?

If the overseas organisation requires a translation, check both its translation rules and the Authentication Unit requirements before arranging one.

For translations organised independently for the Authentication Unit process, current government guidance requires NZ Notary Public notarisation. Translation requirements can therefore affect both the preparation steps and whether an application qualifies for urgent processing.

See our Translation Coordination service.

Common mistakes with education documents

  • Apostilling a degree certificate when the overseas organisation actually wanted the transcript.
  • Paying for notarisation before checking whether it is required.
  • Assuming an apostille proves academic equivalence or recognition.
  • Using a photocopy without checking the Authentication Unit's requirements for that document.
  • Choosing an e-apostille when the receiving institution wants physical documents.
  • Failing to ask whether the university or provider must send the document directly.
  • Starting translation before confirming the required translation and notarisation pathway.

A practical checklist before you start

  1. Identify the destination country.
  2. Ask the receiving organisation for its written document requirements.
  3. Confirm exactly which academic document it wants.
  4. Check whether direct institutional verification is required instead of, or as well as, apostille.
  5. Check DIA preparation requirements for the particular document.
  6. Only then arrange apostille, notary, translation and courier steps that are actually necessary.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an apostille for my New Zealand degree?

Possibly, but not automatically. Ask the overseas organisation what it requires. It may request an apostille, direct verification from the issuing institution, an official transcript, online verification or another process.

Should I apostille my degree or my transcript?

That depends on the receiving organisation. Ask it to specify exactly which document or documents must be authenticated before you apply.

Does an apostille prove my NZ degree is recognised overseas?

No. An apostille confirms the authenticity of the relevant signature, stamp or seal. Recognition or equivalence of the qualification is a separate matter decided by the receiving institution or relevant authority.

Can NZQA help with documents for using my qualification overseas?

Yes. NZQA provides official qualification documents and guidance for people using New Zealand qualifications overseas. Depending on the qualification, your school, university or tertiary provider may also need to supply official records.

How much is a New Zealand apostille?

The current government fee is NZ$37 for one apostille certificate. Other costs can apply for document preparation, notary work, translation, courier services or administrative assistance.

How long does an education-document apostille take?

Current standard apostille processing is within 15 working days. Eligible urgent applications are processed within 5 working days for an additional NZ$120 government urgent-service fee.
