What Are the Requirements for an Apostille in New Zealand?

There is no single set of preparation requirements that applies to every New Zealand document.

The correct process depends on three things:

  • What the document is — for example a birth certificate, university qualification, criminal record, company document or privately prepared document.
  • Which country the document will be used in — because the destination determines whether an apostille or authentication process applies.
  • What the organisation receiving the document requires — because an overseas authority can impose additional conditions.

Apostille or Authentication?

This is one of the first things that needs to be established. If you want a side-by-side explanation first, read our Apostille vs Authentication NZ guide.

An apostille is generally used when the destination falls under the Hague Apostille Convention process. Other destinations may require authentication, sometimes also described as legalisation.

Apostille Authentication
Used for destinations participating in the Hague Apostille Convention process. Used for destinations that require authentication or legalisation instead.
Can be supplied as a paper apostille or an e-apostille. May involve additional verification and, depending on the destination, further overseas authority requirements.

If your destination requires authentication rather than an apostille, see our New Zealand Document Authentication service .

Does My Document Need to Be an Original?

Sometimes yes, but not always.

Depending on the particular document, the required format may involve:

  • the original document
  • a properly notarised copy
  • an accepted electronically issued document
  • additional supporting documentation

The overseas organisation receiving the document may also impose its own conditions. If you are unsure who can certify a copy, see Who Can Certify Documents in NZ?.

Unsure whether to send the original?

Which Types of Documents May Need an Apostille?

Birth Certificates, Marriage Certificates & Death Certificates

Frequently required for overseas marriage, citizenship, family, inheritance, residency and official registration purposes.

Education Documents

Degrees, diplomas, transcripts and other education documents may be requested when studying, working or seeking professional recognition overseas.

Criminal Record Documents

New Zealand criminal record documents can be requested for employment, residency, citizenship or other overseas procedures.

Company Documents

Company records, resolutions and other corporate documents may require preparation before being accepted overseas.

Powers of Attorney

Privately prepared documents such as powers of attorney can involve additional notarisation or execution requirements.

Property & Estate Documents

Overseas property transactions, estates and inheritance matters can require New Zealand documents to be formally prepared for international use.

What If My Document Needs to Be Notarised?

Some documents need to be notarised before they can proceed through the apostille or authentication process.

Where notarisation is specifically required for this process, it must be completed by a New Zealand Notary Public.

A Justice of the Peace or ordinary solicitor certification is not a substitute where notarisation is specifically required.

NZ Document Assist is not a Notary Public. However, we can help coordinate the notarial stage as part of the overall document process.

See our Notary Coordination service .

What About PDFs and Electronic Documents?

A document being supplied as a PDF does not automatically mean it must be printed and notarised.

Some documents are officially issued electronically and can be handled in an accepted electronic format. Others may require additional verification or notarisation.

We look at factors such as:

  • who issued the PDF
  • whether it is an official electronically issued document
  • whether its authenticity can be verified
  • the requirements applying to that document type
  • whether the overseas organisation accepts electronic documents

Paper Apostille vs E-Apostille

New Zealand offers both paper apostilles and electronic apostilles. For a clearer comparison of the two formats, see Paper vs E-Apostille NZ.

Paper Apostille E-Apostille
A physical apostille certificate accompanies the document set. An electronic apostille is supplied as a secure PDF.
Often appropriate where the receiving organisation requires physical documentation. Useful where the receiving organisation accepts electronic documentation.
Physical return or courier arrangements are required. The apostille itself can be delivered electronically.

Do My Documents Need to Be Translated?

Not every overseas application requires a translation.

The organisation receiving your documents may require them to be translated into another language.

Translation requirements are best established before the apostille process begins because the way a translation is prepared can affect the document-handling steps.

We can help coordinate translation requirements through our Translation Coordination service .

Does a Birth or Marriage Certificate Need to Be Recent?

Sometimes the organisation overseas requires a certificate issued within a particular period.

This can arise with citizenship, marriage, residency, registry and other overseas procedures.

If you have been told your certificate must be recently issued, send us those instructions and we can factor them into the coordination process.

Can Several Documents Be Processed Together?

Often people need more than one document prepared for the same overseas matter.

Depending on the documents and the receiving organisation, they may be handled individually or as part of the same overall case.

If you have several documents, send us the complete list. We can look at the administrative requirements together rather than treating each item in isolation. For a broader overview of preparing New Zealand documents internationally, see Documents for Overseas Use.

Current New Zealand Apostille Fee and Processing Time

As at September 2026, the New Zealand Government fee for one paper apostille or e-apostille certificate is NZ$37. Standard apostille applications are currently processed within 15 working days.

An eligible urgent apostille or e-apostille application can currently be processed within 5 working days for an additional NZ$120 urgent service fee. The urgent service has eligibility conditions, including that the application is complete and does not require translation.

Can I Arrange a New Zealand Apostille While Living Overseas?

In many cases, yes.

You do not necessarily need to be physically present in New Zealand simply because your New Zealand document needs an apostille.

Depending on the document, we can help coordinate matters involving:

Common Problems We Help Clients Avoid

Apostille cases can become frustrating when one small requirement is missed. Part of our role is helping coordinate the practical details before they create unnecessary delays.

  • Sending a copy when an original document is required.
  • Using a JP when a New Zealand Notary Public is required.
  • Choosing a paper apostille when the overseas organisation expects an electronic format.
  • Choosing an e-apostille when the receiving organisation wants physical documentation.
  • Discovering too late that a recently issued certificate is required.
  • Arranging translation before understanding how the translated document must be prepared.
  • Assuming every destination follows the same apostille procedure.
  • Not allowing enough time for notarisation, translation, processing and courier delivery.

New Zealand Apostille Requirements — Common Questions

What documents can need an apostille in New Zealand?

Birth and marriage certificates, education documents, criminal record documents, company documents, powers of attorney and many other documents being used overseas can require an apostille.

Do I need the original document?

It depends on the document. Some document types must be originals, while others may be accepted as properly notarised copies or in an approved electronic format.

Can I use a Justice of the Peace?

Not where the document specifically needs to be notarised for the apostille or authentication process. In that situation, the notarisation must be completed by a New Zealand Notary Public.

Is an e-apostille the same as a paper apostille?

Both are official apostille formats. A paper apostille accompanies the physical document set while an e-apostille is supplied electronically as a secure PDF. The receiving organisation should confirm which format it accepts.

Does an e-apostille mean I do not have to send the original?

Not necessarily. The apostille format and the requirements applying to the underlying document are separate issues.

Can I arrange a New Zealand apostille from overseas?

In many cases, yes. We can help coordinate the process using email, post and courier services, depending on the document requirements.

How long should I allow?

Standard apostille processing is currently within 15 working days. Eligible urgent applications can currently be processed within 5 working days. Additional time may be required for notarisation, translation and delivery.

What if I do not know what the overseas organisation wants?

Send us the document type, destination country and any emails, forms or instructions you have received. We can help identify the practical administrative requirements that need to be checked.
