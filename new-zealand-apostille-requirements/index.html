What Are the Requirements for an Apostille in New Zealand?

There is no single set of preparation requirements that applies to every New Zealand document.

The correct process depends on three things:

  • What the document is — for example a birth certificate, university qualification, criminal record, company document or privately prepared document.
  • Which country the document will be used in — because the destination determines whether an apostille or authentication process applies.
  • What the organisation receiving the document requires — because an overseas authority can impose additional conditions.
Quick answer:

Before arranging an apostille, establish the destination country, confirm what format the receiving organisation will accept, and check whether your particular document must be supplied as an original, notarised copy or accepted electronic document.

Apostille or Authentication?

This is the first thing that needs to be established.

An apostille is used when the destination falls under the Hague Apostille Convention process. Other destinations may require authentication, sometimes also described as legalisation.

Apostille Authentication
Used for destinations participating in the Hague Apostille Convention process. Used for destinations that require authentication or legalisation instead.
Can be supplied as a paper apostille or an e-apostille. May involve additional checks by MFAT and, depending on the destination, an embassy or consular authority.
Generally a more direct process once the document meets the required criteria. Category B authentication can involve more stages and does not have the same predictable processing timeframe.

If your country requires authentication rather than an apostille, read our New Zealand Document Authentication guide .

Which Countries Commonly Use a New Zealand Apostille?

Many common destinations for New Zealand documents currently use the apostille process.

Examples of Apostille Destinations

These include many countries where New Zealanders commonly need documents for work, study, marriage, residency, property or family matters.

Australia Canada China France Germany India Italy Japan New Zealand documents for UK use Philippines Portugal Serbia South Korea Spain United States

Country requirements can change, and the receiving organisation may have requirements beyond simply obtaining an apostille.

If you are unsure, tell us the destination country when you contact us.

Does My Document Need to Be an Original?

Sometimes yes, but not always.

The Authentication Unit can only process documents that meet the requirements for that particular document type. Depending on the document, this can mean supplying:

  • the original document
  • a properly notarised copy
  • an accepted electronically issued document
  • additional supporting documentation

The overseas organisation can also impose its own conditions. For example, it may insist on an original even if another form of the document could otherwise be processed.

Do not assume a photocopy will be enough.

Check the requirements before sending a valuable original document or paying to have copies certified.

Which Types of Documents May Need an Apostille?

Birth, Marriage & Death Certificates

Frequently required for overseas marriage, citizenship, family, inheritance, residency and official registration purposes.

Education Documents

Degrees, diplomas, transcripts and other education documents may be requested when studying, working or seeking professional recognition overseas.

Criminal Record Documents

New Zealand criminal record documents can be requested for employment, residency, citizenship or other overseas procedures.

Company Documents

Company records, resolutions and other corporate documents may require preparation before being accepted overseas.

Powers of Attorney

Privately prepared documents such as powers of attorney often involve additional notarisation or execution requirements.

Property & Estate Documents

Overseas property sales, purchases, estates and inheritance matters can require New Zealand documents to be formally prepared for international use.

What If My Document Needs to Be Notarised?

Some documents need to be notarised before they can be apostilled or authenticated.

Where notarisation is required for the New Zealand apostille or authentication process, it must be completed by a New Zealand Notary Public.

A Justice of the Peace or ordinary solicitor certification is not a substitute where notarisation is specifically required for this process.

The notarial act normally records what the notary has done and includes the notary's name, signature, seal or stamp, date and place of signing.

NZ Document Assist is not a Notary Public, but we can help coordinate this stage. See our Notary Coordination service .

What About PDFs and Electronic Documents?

A document being supplied as a PDF does not automatically mean it must be printed and notarised.

Some documents are officially issued electronically and can be dealt with in an accepted electronic format. Other PDF documents may need additional verification or notarisation before they can proceed.

The key questions are:

  • Who issued the PDF?
  • Is it an official electronically issued document?
  • Can its authenticity be verified?
  • What does the Authentication Unit require for that document type?
  • Will the overseas organisation accept the electronic format?

Paper Apostille vs E-Apostille

New Zealand offers two apostille formats.

Paper Apostille E-Apostille
A physical apostille certificate is attached to the document set. A secure electronic apostille is supplied as a PDF.
Useful where the overseas organisation requires physical documents. Useful where the receiving organisation accepts electronic documentation.
Physical return or courier arrangements are required. The e-apostille itself can be delivered electronically.
Important:

Choosing an e-apostille does not automatically mean that your underlying document can be supplied electronically. The document itself must still satisfy the applicable requirements.

Do My Documents Need to Be Translated?

Not every overseas application requires a translation.

The organisation receiving your documents may require them to be translated into its local language.

Translation requirements should ideally be established before the apostille process starts because the translation itself can affect how the documents need to be prepared.

Translation can be arranged as part of the official application process, or independently.

If an independent translation is being used for the apostille or authentication process, additional notarisation requirements can apply.

We can help coordinate this through our Translation Coordination service .

Does a Birth or Marriage Certificate Need to Be Recent?

This is an important distinction.

An overseas authority can require a certificate that was issued within a particular period — even where the New Zealand apostille process itself does not create the same age requirement.

For example, an overseas registry, citizenship authority or other organisation may ask for a recently issued birth or marriage certificate.

Always check the instructions of the organisation that will ultimately receive the document.

Can Several Documents Be Processed Together?

Sometimes, but this depends on the documents and how they are intended to be used.

An overseas organisation may want several documents presented together, while another may require them to be treated separately.

Related documents or other items can sometimes also accompany the apostilled or authenticated documents when they are sent onward.

It is important to make clear exactly which documents require the apostille or authentication.

How Long Does the Apostille Process Take?

Standard apostille processing is currently within 15 working days once a complete application meeting the required criteria has been received.

Eligible urgent apostille and e-apostille applications can currently be processed within 5 working days.

An additional NZ$120 urgent-processing fee applies.

To qualify for urgent processing, the application must be complete and correct, contain all required documents and must not require translation.

Planning for a deadline?

Allow extra time for notarisation, translation, courier delivery and any overseas embassy or organisation requirements. The processing period is only one part of the complete document journey.

Can I Arrange a New Zealand Apostille While Living Overseas?

In many cases, yes.

You do not necessarily need to be physically present in New Zealand simply because your New Zealand document needs an apostille.

Depending on the document, the process can often be coordinated using:

  • email
  • electronic documents where permitted
  • international courier
  • New Zealand postal or courier handling
  • notary coordination where required
  • return delivery arrangements

This is one of the situations where an administrative coordination service can be particularly useful because several organisations and delivery stages may be involved.

Common Mistakes That Can Delay an Apostille

  • Sending a copy when the document needs to be an original.
  • Having a document certified by a JP when a New Zealand Notary Public is required.
  • Obtaining an e-apostille when the overseas organisation wants paper.
  • Ordering a paper apostille when the receiving organisation expects an electronic document.
  • Discovering too late that the overseas organisation requires a recently issued certificate.
  • Arranging a translation before establishing how the translated document must be prepared.
  • Assuming every country uses the apostille process.
  • Failing to allow enough time for notarisation, translation, courier delivery or overseas requirements.

New Zealand Apostille Requirements — Common Questions

What documents can need an apostille in New Zealand?

Birth and marriage certificates, education documents, criminal record documents, company documents, powers of attorney and many other documents being used overseas can require an apostille. Requirements depend on the document and the organisation receiving it.

Do I need the original document?

It depends on the document. Some document types must be originals, while others may be accepted as properly notarised copies or in an approved electronic format.

Can I use a Justice of the Peace?

Not where the document specifically needs to be notarised for the apostille or authentication process. In that situation, the notarisation must be completed by a New Zealand Notary Public.

Is an e-apostille the same as a paper apostille?

Both are official apostille formats. The main practical difference is that a paper apostille is physically attached to the document package while an e-apostille is supplied electronically as a secure PDF. The receiving organisation should confirm which it accepts.

Does an e-apostille mean I do not have to send the original?

Not necessarily. The apostille format and the underlying document requirements are separate issues. Your document may still need to be supplied in its required original or other accepted form.

Can I arrange a New Zealand apostille from overseas?

In many cases, yes. The process can often be coordinated by email, post and courier, depending on the document requirements.

How long should I allow?

Standard apostille processing is currently within 15 working days. Eligible urgent applications can currently be processed within 5 working days, but you should also allow time for any notarisation, translation and delivery stages.

What if I do not know what the overseas organisation wants?

Send us the document type, destination country and any emails, forms or instructions you have received. That information is often enough to identify what administrative steps need to be checked next.
