When Might a Document Need Translation?

A receiving organisation overseas may ask for a translation when a document is not in the language it accepts.

This can arise with documents connected with matters such as:

  • birth, marriage and death records
  • education and qualification documents
  • police or criminal record documents
  • powers of attorney and other personal documents
  • business or company documents
  • overseas property or inheritance matters
  • documents being supplied to foreign authorities or organisations
Important:

The organisation receiving your document determines what type of translation it will accept. Requirements can differ between countries, authorities and document types.

Translation With an Apostille or Authentication

Translation is sometimes only one stage of an overseas document process. Depending on the instructions you have received, a document may also require an apostille or authentication.

We can coordinate the practical workflow between these stages where appropriate, rather than leaving you to organise each administrative step separately.

What If a Notary Public Is Required?

Some document processes may require a notarial step. Where that applies, the notarial act is performed independently by a New Zealand Notary Public.

NZ Document Assist is not a Notary Public, but we can help coordinate a notary appointment and continue managing the surrounding administrative document process where agreed.
