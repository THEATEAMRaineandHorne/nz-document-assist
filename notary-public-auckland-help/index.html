What Does a Notary Public Do in New Zealand?

A New Zealand Notary Public is a lawyer authorised to perform specialised notarial acts.

Depending on the circumstances, a notary may:

  • witness signatures on documents
  • certify the authenticity of certain documents
  • certify copies for overseas use
  • collect sworn statements
  • administer oaths
  • perform other notarial acts intended for use overseas

The exact notarial act required depends on the document and the requirements of the organisation receiving it overseas.

Why Would I Need a Notary Public?

A notary is commonly required when a foreign authority, overseas lawyer, bank, company, property professional or other organisation needs additional formal verification of a New Zealand document or signature.

Powers of Attorney

Overseas property, financial or family matters can require a power of attorney or related signature to be notarised.

Certified Copies

An overseas organisation may specifically require a copy to be certified by a Notary Public rather than another type of certifier.

Company Documents

Corporate documents, resolutions and signatures can require notarial verification for international transactions.

Property Documents

Documents connected with overseas property purchases, sales or ownership matters can require notarisation.

Affidavits & Declarations

Certain documents intended for use overseas can require a formal witnessed or sworn process.

Other Overseas Documents

Requirements vary widely, so it is important to understand exactly what the receiving organisation has requested.

Notary Public vs Justice of the Peace

A Justice of the Peace and a Notary Public are not interchangeable.

Justice of the Peace Notary Public
Can perform various witnessing and certification functions within the scope of JP services. A lawyer authorised to perform specialised notarial acts, particularly for documents intended for international use.
May be suitable where the organisation specifically accepts JP certification. Required where the receiving organisation or document process specifically asks for notarisation.
Cannot substitute for a Notary Public where notarisation is specifically required. Can perform the notarial act required for relevant overseas document processes.
If your instructions specifically say Notary Public, notarised or notarial certification, do not assume ordinary JP certification will be sufficient.

Does My Document Need to Be Signed in Front of the Notary?

Sometimes.

If the notary needs to witness your signature, you should generally avoid signing the document beforehand unless the notary has told you otherwise.

Other cases may involve certifying a copy or verifying an existing document rather than witnessing a new signature.

This is one reason we ask clients to send us the document type and the instructions they have received before the appointment is coordinated.

What Should I Take to a Notary Appointment?

The exact requirements depend on the notarial act, but formal identification is commonly required.

This can include:

  • a current passport or other acceptable photo identification
  • the original document being notarised
  • original identity documents referred to in the document
  • supporting documents requested by the notary
  • instructions from the overseas receiving organisation

We can help coordinate the administrative preparation before the appointment so you know what practical items need to be checked.

Does a Notarised Document Also Need an Apostille?

Sometimes it does.

Notarisation and apostille are two different stages.

The Notary Public performs the notarial act. If an apostille is also required, the document may then need to proceed through the New Zealand apostille process before being used overseas.

You can read more about this on our Apostille NZ service page and our New Zealand Apostille Requirements guide .

What If the Country Requires Authentication Instead?

For some destination countries, authentication or legalisation is required instead of an apostille.

In those cases, notarisation may be only the first stage of a longer process.

The document may then need to move through New Zealand authentication stages and, in some cases, further embassy processing.

See our Document Authentication NZ service .

Do I Need a Notary for Every Apostille?

No.

Many New Zealand documents can proceed through the apostille process without first being notarised.

Whether notarisation is required depends on the document type and how it needs to be presented.

What If My Document Needs Translation?

Overseas authorities may also require a translation.

The order in which notarisation, translation and apostille or authentication are completed can matter, so it is useful to understand the receiving organisation's requirements first.

Notary Public Auckland — Common Questions

Is NZ Document Assist a Notary Public?

No. NZ Document Assist is an independent administrative document assistance service. We coordinate appointments with independent New Zealand Notaries Public and can help manage related overseas document processing.

What does a Notary Public do in New Zealand?

A New Zealand Notary Public is a lawyer authorised to perform notarial acts such as witnessing signatures, certifying documents, collecting sworn statements and administering oaths, including for documents intended for overseas use.

Can I use a JP instead of a Notary Public?

Not if the organisation or process specifically requires a Notary Public. JP certification and notarisation are different services.

Should I sign my document before seeing the notary?

If your signature needs to be witnessed, you generally should not sign beforehand unless the notary has specifically instructed you to do so.

What ID should I take?

Formal photo identification such as a passport or driver licence is commonly required. The notary may also need to sight original documents referred to in the document being notarised.

Does notarisation include the apostille?

No. They are different stages. If an apostille is required after notarisation, that must be handled through the New Zealand apostille process.

Do all documents need to be notarised before an apostille?

No. Whether notarisation is required depends on the document and the applicable requirements.

Can you organise the apostille after my notary appointment?

Yes, where an apostille is the appropriate next stage, NZ Document Assist can coordinate the administrative apostille process and document handling for you.
