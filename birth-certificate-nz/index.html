Two documents, only one of them legal

Births, Deaths and Marriages can issue two quite different things from the same birth entry, and they are easy to mix up when ordering.

The birth certificate

The legal document. It is what passport offices, schools, banks, employers and overseas authorities expect, and the one that gets apostilled when it has to be used abroad.

What we order

A copy of the birth record

A reproduction of the register entry. It can carry more detail than the certificate, which makes it useful for family history, but it is not a legal document.

Research only

Four details on the certificate that cause trouble

These come up again and again in our specialist's experience, and each one can mean a second order and a second wait if it isn't caught first.

The issue date

Old certificate, new rules

A birth certificate never expires, but many visa and immigration offices only accept one issued in the last 6 or 12 months. We had a client whose certificate was eight months old against a six-month rule: here's how we caught it.

The surname

Born before 1972?

Anyone born before 1972 was registered with no surname at all. When the gaps were filled in 1995, the surname chosen hung on one question, whether the parents were married. If what was chosen isn't the name you've used all your life, the record needs amending before a new certificate will show the right name.

The name

Changed your name?

A name change registered in New Zealand sits against your birth entry, so the certificate can reflect it. Born overseas and changed your name here? Then it's a name change certificate you need, not a birth certificate.

The parents

Adopted?

The certificate issued after an adoption shows the adoptive parents. The original, pre-adoption certificate is a separate request, available to the adopted person once they turn 20.

Ordering for someone else

Parents can order for their children, and adults can order their own. For anyone else we need to know you're entitled to ask: written authority from the person, a power of attorney, or, for someone who has died, the document appointing you as executor or administrator. Births, Deaths and Marriages keeps a record of who requested each certificate, so this part is never skipped. Births from 100 or more years ago are held as historical records. Tell us the year and we'll say what can be issued before you pay.

Lost it, damaged it, or something's wrong on it?

Lost or damaged is simple: every certificate is freshly issued from the register, so a replacement is just a new order. Wrong is different. If a name, date or parent's detail on the register is incorrect, ordering another certificate will print the same mistake. The register has to be corrected first. Tell us what's wrong and we'll tell you which it is before you spend anything.

Not born in New Zealand?

We can only order births registered in New Zealand. A birth overseas is registered, and certified, by that country. If you're the child of a New Zealander and were born abroad, registering New Zealand citizenship by descent is a separate application, but the New Zealand documents it leans on, such as a parent's birth certificate or the parents' marriage certificate, are ones we can order for you.

Questions people ask us

How quickly can I get a New Zealand birth certificate?

Most certificates reach a New Zealand address within one to two weeks of ordering. If something is waiting on it, such as a passport appointment or a visa lodgement date, put that date in your order and we will tell you straight away whether it is realistic.

Is an NZ birth certificate proof of New Zealand citizenship?

Not always. For anyone born in New Zealand before 2006, birth here generally meant citizenship. From 1 January 2006 it depends on whether a parent was a citizen or entitled to live here indefinitely at the time of the birth. If you are using the certificate for a passport or citizenship matter, tell us the birth date and we will flag whether separate proof of citizenship may be asked for.

My birth certificate is years old. Is it still valid?

A New Zealand birth certificate does not expire. The catch is that some overseas offices, most often visa and immigration offices, only accept a certificate issued within the last 6 or 12 months. In that case you need a newly issued certificate, even though nothing on it has changed.

Can you order a birth certificate for my parent or grandparent?

Usually, yes. We can order with their written authority, under a power of attorney, or for an estate when you are the executor or administrator. If the birth was 100 or more years ago, it is held as a historical record and we will tell you what can be issued before you pay.

Certificate or copy of the record: which one will an office accept?

The certificate is the legal document. A copy of the record reproduces the register entry and can show more detail, but it is meant for family history and is not a legal document. Passport offices, schools, banks and overseas authorities want the certificate, and that is what we order unless you ask otherwise.

I changed my name. Will my new name be on my birth certificate?

A name change registered in New Zealand is recorded against your birth entry, so a newly issued certificate can reflect it. If you were born overseas and changed your name here, the document you need is a name change certificate instead. We confirm which one fits before ordering.

Do I need an apostille on my birth certificate?

Only if it is going to an authority in another country that asks for one. For New Zealand use, such as a passport, school or KiwiSaver, the certificate on its own is what is needed. If it is going overseas, we can order, apostille or authenticate and courier it in one job.

Can you send the certificate to me overseas?

Yes. The NZ$79 price covers posting within New Zealand. If you are overseas, ask for an all-in price that includes courier to your country, and an apostille if the receiving office wants one.
