Auckland apostille assistance without the paperwork headache

NZ Document Assist provides independent administrative help for Auckland clients preparing New Zealand documents for overseas use. Depending on your situation, we can coordinate the apostille application and help organise related notary, translation and document-return requirements.

The government apostille itself is issued by the Department of Internal Affairs Authentication Unit. Our role is to help you understand what is likely to be required and coordinate the administrative process so you are not left trying to piece everything together yourself.

What documents can we help with?

We can assist with many common New Zealand documents used overseas, including birth and marriage certificates, criminal records, degrees and education documents, powers of attorney, divorce or dissolution papers, passport-copy requirements, Certificates of No Impediment and other eligible New Zealand documents.

Do you actually need an apostille?

Possibly — but not every overseas document uses the apostille pathway. The correct process depends mainly on the destination country and the receiving organisation's requirements. Some documents need authentication instead, and some need preparation such as notarisation or translation first.

This is why we recommend contacting us before arranging a JP, notary, translation or other certification. We can help you avoid starting with the wrong step.

Do you need a Notary Public first?

Some documents do and some do not. Where the Department of Internal Affairs requires notarisation for apostille or authentication, current government rules require a New Zealand Notary Public — not a Justice of the Peace or ordinary solicitor certification.

If notarisation is required, we can help coordinate that step. You can also read our JP vs Notary Public NZ guide.

How much does apostille help cost?

Our Apostille Coordination service starts from NZ$89, with government and other third-party costs separate unless expressly included in your written quote.

The current government fee for one paper or electronic apostille certificate is NZ$37. Eligible urgent processing currently costs an additional NZ$120 government fee.

Before you spend money on unnecessary steps, contact us for help with your particular documents.

How long does an apostille take?

Current government standard apostille processing is within 15 working days. Eligible urgent apostille and e-apostille applications are processed within 5 working days. Other steps — such as obtaining documents, notarisation, translation or courier delivery — can add time.

We cannot make a government or third-party process faster, but we can help coordinate the steps and reduce avoidable delays caused by starting with the wrong document or process.
