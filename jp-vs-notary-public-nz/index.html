JP vs Notary Public in NZ: Which One Do You Need?

A JP is free. A notary is not. Here is how to tell which one your document needs, so you do not pay twice or get a document refused.

Quick answer

Use a Justice of the Peace for certified copies and witnessing in New Zealand when the receiving organisation accepts a JP: it is free. Use a Notary Public when a document is going overseas and the instructions say “notarised”, or when DIA requires notarisation before an apostille. DIA refuses JP and ordinary solicitor certification in those cases. A notary typically charges NZ$100–175.

When a JP is enough

  • A NZ bank, school, employer or agency asks for a certified copy and lists JPs as accepted
  • A statutory declaration or affidavit for New Zealand use
  • An overseas office says in writing that it accepts a JP certification and does not want an apostille

When you need a Notary Public

  • The instructions say “notarised” or “notarial certificate”
  • DIA requires notarisation before the apostille: powers of attorney, school documents, wills, deeds, employment and medical records, private documents and your own translations
  • A passport copy needs an apostille or authentication

When you need neither

Birth, death, marriage and name change certificates, criminal records and NZQA records go to DIA as originals. Paying a JP or notary to certify them adds cost and DIA will not accept the copy. See NZ Apostille Requirements.

Is a lawyer the same as a notary?

No. Every Notary Public in New Zealand is a lawyer, but most lawyers are not notaries. If the instructions require notarisation, an ordinary solicitor's certification will be refused.

Mistakes that cost money

  • Getting a JP certification when the instructions say “notarised”
  • Paying a notary for a BDM certificate that only needs the original
  • Assuming any lawyer can notarise
  • Confusing notarisation with an apostille: the apostille comes after, from DIA

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a JP and a Notary Public in NZ?

A JP is a free community volunteer who certifies copies and witnesses documents for New Zealand use. A Notary Public is an experienced lawyer who performs formal notarial acts, mainly for documents going overseas, and charges a fee.

Can a JP notarise a document in NZ?

No. Only a Notary Public can notarise. A JP can certify copies and witness signatures where that is accepted.

Does DIA accept a JP certification for an apostille?

No. Where DIA requires notarisation before an apostille or authentication, it must be a NZ Notary Public. JP and ordinary solicitor certifications are refused.

How much does a Notary Public cost in NZ?

Notaries set their own fees, typically NZ$100 to NZ$175 for a straightforward notarisation. We book the appointment for NZ$39.

Is every lawyer a Notary Public?

No. Notaries are lawyers with a separate appointment. Most lawyers are not notaries.

Do I need a notary for a birth certificate apostille?

No. DIA apostilles the original BDM birth certificate directly, with no JP or notary.
